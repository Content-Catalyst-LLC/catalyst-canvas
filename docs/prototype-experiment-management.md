# Prototype and Experiment Management

Catalyst Canvas v1.9.0 turns a prototype from a single concept description into a traceable learning object.

## Prototype portfolio

Each prototype has a stable identifier, type, fidelity, lifecycle status, version, owner, linked ideas and decision options, assumptions, evidence, artifacts, success definition, limitations, and timestamps.

## Hypotheses

Hypotheses must be written as testable statements and can include a falsification condition. Their lifecycle distinguishes draft, active, supported, partially supported, not supported, inconclusive, and retired states.

## Experiment planning

A plan records what will be learned, how the test will run, who will participate, what will be measured, what threshold will count as a signal, and what safeguards or stop conditions apply. Unknown or missing fields remain visible in `experiment_summary`.

## Running and reviewing experiments

A run is a historical record rather than an overwrite of the plan. It preserves observed metric values, sample sizes, confidence, evidence, observations, limitations, and incidents. A separate learning decision records whether the team will continue, iterate, pivot, stop, escalate, or retest.

## Iteration history

Prototype changes preserve the prior and next version, change summary, reason, experiment runs, learning decision, author, and timestamp. This creates a reviewable path from test result to changed artifact.

## Institutional handoffs

Research Lab receives execution context, participant plans, safeguards, datasets, and compute requirements. Workbench receives metric definitions, calculation requirements, modeling questions, and prototype artifacts. Both receive the Canvas context, evidence, assumptions, results, and provenance.
